How it works

  1. 1

    Capture or describe

    Take a photo or type “2 injera with shiro and an orange”.

  2. 2

    Nourish identifies the food

    Each candidate is matched to a canonical Ethiopian food record — allergies to spelling, transliteration and Amharic alike.

  3. 3

    You confirm or adjust

    Nothing is saved until you agree. Change the portion, swap a dish, remove what you did not eat.

  4. 4

    Deterministic nutrition

    Calories and macros are computed as per-100 g × grams ÷ 100 from the published table — not estimated by a model.

Food should be easy to track.

From injera and shiro to tibs and kitfo, Nourish is designed around the foods you actually eat. Every value is traceable to its source row, with the publication, the food code and the import date recorded alongside it.

Questions

Is the calorie count exact?

No, and it never claims to be. Portions you or the camera estimate carry uncertainty, so estimated figures are shown with a “~”. What is exact is the arithmetic: the app computes per-100 g × grams ÷ 100 from published values.

Which foods are covered?

The app ships the condensed table of the Ethiopian Food Composition Table 2025 (EPHI & FAO) — 722 published food rows — plus standard portion measures for Ethiopian dishes.

What happens to my meal photos?

They are sent for analysis and are not stored by the service. The app keeps your meal record, not the image.

Can I use it without a connection?

Yes. Search, logging, water and history work offline from the on-device catalogue, which updates when you are online.

When is iOS available?

Android ships first. iOS needs Apple signing and distribution approval; until that exists, we say so rather than offering a file that cannot be installed.
